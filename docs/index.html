THE QUESTION BEHIND THE LAB

Can a system that predicts us
make us more alike?

The paper shows how predictive feedback can reduce disagreement and, under stated assumptions, drive a network toward consensus—even when ordinary peer dynamics preserve different opinions.

The lab distinguishes stability from agreement: a simulation can stop changing while people still hold different views. “Near consensus” here means an opinion range below 0.01.

Read the theoretical results ↗
Methods, assumptions, and reproducibility +

What follows the paper

The nested update order, Friedkin–Johnsen peer dynamics, perfect prediction (Eq. 5), observed-mean extrapolation (Eq. 8), and single-person prediction intervention (Eq. 10) follow the paper. Predictions are initialized by applying the selected policy to the innate opinions.

W is the row-normalized adjacency matrix. All peer updates are simultaneous. For K → ∞, the lab solves the FJ linear system. The dashed reference can use the same K or the full platform-free FJ equilibrium. Both use the same graph and innate opinions.

What is exploratory

Networks, innate opinions, and regression features are synthetic. These runs do not reproduce the Yelp or Pokec experiments. Custom inputs select the model’s innate opinions. Disconnected networks and ridge regularization provide extensions for exploration.

Linear prediction fits observed people using fixed synthetic features with an intercept, then clips predictions to [0, 1]. The Methods section in an exported experiment records the actual feature and sampling conventions.

Reading the results

The clean consensus result assumes a connected graph, strictly positive peer susceptibility below 1, perfect prediction, and uniform platform influence approaching 1. The variance-monotonicity proposition additionally assumes a regular graph and homogeneous susceptibilities.

For the affine policies, the lab solves for the long-run state separately from the displayed trajectory. Closed classes use their stationary average of the innate opinions to select the limit from this initialization. The clipped regression policy reports the simulated endpoint and its next-round change. Near consensus means an opinion range below 0.01.

Repeat or inspect an experiment

Graph, data, observation, and susceptibility sampling use seeded random streams. Keep the seed fixed while adjusting influence to hold the graph and innate data constant. Ordinary graph generators add joining edges when needed for connectivity. Isolated people in exploratory disconnected graphs treat their own opinion as their peer average.
